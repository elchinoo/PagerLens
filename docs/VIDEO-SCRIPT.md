# PagerLens customer video script (problem first)

**Title:** Find the likely cause in minutes, and let the next incident learn: a sample app on Postgres with AgentPG

**Target length:** about 7 and a half minutes as built by `docs/video/build.sh` (narration about 1,050 words in six Kokoro voices, a 3.5 second title card, deliberate pauses over on-screen waits and a short hold after every scene). The build drives every scene from the measured length of its audio (each scene lasts at least its narration plus 0.7 seconds), so the picture matches what is being said.

**Audience:** customers and their engineers (SREs, platform and database people). One of five customer videos; the internal set is separate.

**The question the video answers** (from review: "show the EDB tech on screen; I keep missing the what: I am trying to do X, let me show you how"):

| I am trying to... | Postgres / AgentPG capability shown | Where in the video |
|---|---|---|
| find the likely cause of a 3 a.m. incident from logs, metrics and runbooks in one place | one Postgres holds telemetry, runbooks and memory; read-only investigator | scenes 1 to 5 |
| have the next incident learn from this one | pgvector `embedding <=> ...` with an HNSW index (`src/pagerlens/retrieval.py`, `001_init.sql`) | scenes 6 and 7 (real SQL, real plan, real lookup) |
| find the right runbook for a symptom | one query that fuses full-text (GIN) and vector (HNSW) rankings by reciprocal rank fusion | scene 7 (real SQL, real result) |
| have the screen update itself when an incident opens | the `tcn` extension: `NOTIFY pl_changes` on every incident and alert row change, one LISTEN connection, server-sent events | scene 8 (real psql `LISTEN`, real notification payload, the list updating itself) |
| catch a slow query in another app's database | `db_insights_top_query_mean_seconds` gauge (from `edb_stat_monitor`, else `pg_stat_statements`), the `SlowQuery` alert and the `slow_query` finding | scene 9 |
| let an assistant or contractor read incidents but not personal data or secrets | `agentpg db-users create --permission read` (group role `a1p_readonly`), masked views, revoked secret tables, enforced by Postgres (`src/pagerlens/hardening.py`) | scene 10 (real read-only login role) |
| see what the database is doing and what to index | the Database tab: `edb_stat_monitor`, `edb_pg_tuner`, `query_advisor`; `agentpg recommend` | scene 11 (local `pg_stat_statements` live; AgentPG output as a labelled capture) |

**What is platform, scaffolding or sample app** (shown as a diagram in scene 2 and as a legend on the end card, in three colours):

- PLATFORM: Postgres on AgentPG (EDB): the instance, extensions (pgvector, pg_trgm, tcn, edb_stat_monitor, query_advisor), db-users, the restricted MCP.
- REFERENCE SCAFFOLDING (swappable): sign-in (any standard OpenID Connect provider, as long as it sends the roles our apps expect), the model gateway (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint), the secret vault (one small module of the shared kit; its key comes from the platform, so it is replaced through the kit, not through a setting).
- SAMPLE APP: PagerLens, and the apps it watches.

## Honesty rules (what is real, what is captured, what is a stand-in)

- Everything in scenes 3 to 10 is a real run against a throwaway local Postgres 17 (pgvector, pg_trgm, tcn, pg_stat_statements) and the real app. Terminal cards that run SQL run real `psql`; the SQL shown for the app's own queries is copied from `src/pagerlens/retrieval.py` and marked "abridged".
- The AI run uses the labelled stand-in model `scripts/fake_llm.py`. It is not a real model and AI accuracy on live models is not measured.
- Scene 7 turns sequential scans off for the plan, because the table has a few rows; the card and the narration say so.
- Scene 9: the gauge is the real `/metrics` of the local PagerLens (source `pg_stat_statements`). The Lab writes the `slowq_*` points that the agent would have pushed (synthetic values; the finding, the evidence line and the investigator are real code).
- Scene 10: the local database has a group role `a1p_readonly` and a login role `pl_reader` created by `docs/video/stack.sh` as a stand-in for what AgentPG provides; the `agentpg db-users create` line is shown as a comment and is not run.
- Scene 11: the local Database tab shows `pg_stat_statements` only. The AgentPG extension list and `agentpg recommend` output are real captures from the live fleet taken on 2026-10-28 (`videos/live-captures`), labelled "CAPTURED ... not run now". The AgentPG version of the page itself is not shown. `edb_pg_tuner` is not in the captured extension list of the PagerLens instance, hence "where installed".
- The runbook search is full-text plus vector. `pg_trgm` is installed but the runbook query does not use it, so the video does not claim trigram search.

## 3. Narration only (for the voice recording, no stage directions)

This is the text of `docs/video/narration.txt`, the source of the synthetic voices (Kokoro, US accent). It is written for listening: short sentences, one idea each. Voices alternate female and male and change with the topic, never mid-scene and never the same voice twice in a row: 1 af_bella, 2 am_michael, 3 af_heart, 4 am_eric, 5 af_sarah, 6 am_adam, 7 af_bella, 8 am_michael, 9 af_heart, 10 am_eric, 11 af_sarah, 12 am_adam. In the file, a blank line is a pause, `...` a beat and `[pause N]` a deliberate silence over something happening on screen. `narration.py sync` (run by the build) fails if this section differs from the file.

1. It is three in the morning. Checkout is failing. Your logs are in one tool, your metrics in another, and your runbooks in a wiki. You need the likely cause in minutes. And you want the next incident to learn from this one. Here is how Postgres on AgentPG does that.
2. Who is who? PagerLens is the sample app. Three things around it are scaffolding you can swap: sign-in, the model gateway, and the secret vault. Sign-in works with any standard OpenID Connect provider, as long as it sends the roles our apps expect. The gateway is any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. The vault is one small module of the shared kit. Its key comes from the platform, so you replace it through the kit, not through a setting. The platform part is Postgres on AgentPG, with its extensions.
3. Now the problem itself. This is the Lab, a simulator. It can break a fictional shop in ten ways. I pick a bad release, and press Break it. An alert fires for the checkout error rate. Now I ask why. I use the Rules method. That is a fixed investigator, with no model and no cost. Done, in seconds. It asked read-only questions about checkout, and what it depends on. Metrics against a baseline. Grouped errors. Recent deploys. And slow queries. Every step is recorded in the run viewer. The answer is a ranked list of causes.
4. Why trust the top answer? It says a release was deployed just before the errors began. Every line is evidence, and it either supports the answer or refutes it. Open one, and you see the raw observation behind it. Here, a new error signature appeared only after the deploy. Log text is untrusted. PagerLens shows it, and never obeys it. Below is the report, in Markdown, ready for a ticket. The runbook steps are only suggestions. PagerLens cannot restart or roll back anything.
5. Now the same incident, with a language model. The method is Rules plus AI. The model drives the same read-only tools. Today the model is a stand-in that ships with the repository. It is not a real model. So no key is needed, and the screens read the same as with a real one. Each model turn shows up in the run viewer, between the tool calls. Both answers blame the release, so confidence goes up. Anything the model claims is checked against real evidence. We have not measured accuracy on live models.
6. Now, the next incident should learn from this one. I confirm the real cause, a bad deploy, and add a note. PagerLens stores this incident as a vector in Postgres. Now I break the same thing again, and investigate. The new report lists the earlier incident, and the cause a person confirmed. Nothing is learned from guesses. How does that lookup work?
7. Under the hood. This is the memory lookup, as plain sequel. It sorts resolved incidents by cosine distance, with the pgvector operator. An HNSW index serves that ordering. These are the real index definitions from this database. Here is a real query plan. This table is tiny, so I turn off sequential scans, to show the index can serve it. And here is the real lookup for our new incident. The earlier one comes back, with its similarity. Runbooks use a hybrid search. One query runs full-text search and vector search, and fuses both rankings. Here is the real result, for the question: checkout errors after a release.
8. Next, live updates. The incident list should change by itself. The tcn extension makes Postgres send a notification whenever an incident or an alert changes. Here I listen to that channel in psql. Now I send an alert, the way any service would, with curl. Two notifications arrive, one for the incident and one for the alert. The payload is only a table and a row number. Never row contents. The app holds one listening connection, and pushes those events to the browser. Watch the list. I send an alert for another service. The new incident appears by itself.
9. Now a quieter failure. No deploy, no outage. One query got slower. Every app on the kit exports a gauge of its slowest statements. On AgentPG it comes from edb_stat_monitor. On this local Postgres it comes from pg_stat_statements. This is the real gauge. PagerLens's agent scrapes it. When a statement gets three times slower than its history, it opens a slow query alert. The Lab scenario writes the points that the agent would have pushed. I break it, and investigate. The top cause is a query that became forty times slower. The evidence names the query, with its mean before and now. The statement text itself is in the Database tab, under that query id.
10. Now, who may read this data? An assistant should see incidents, but not personal data or secrets. On AgentPG, a read-only user comes from one command. It is a member of a read-only group role. Locally, I created a role set up the same way. As the owner, a note holds an email address. As the read-only role, the masked view shows a placeholder instead. The base table is denied. A write is denied too. And the tables with secrets, like token hashes, are denied, with no view at all. Postgres enforces this, not the application code.
11. One more place to look: the Database tab. On this local Postgres it shows only pg_stat_statements, the top statements. On AgentPG, the same page also shows edb_stat_monitor, edb_pg_tuner where installed, and query_advisor. I cannot show that page here. This is real output, captured from our live AgentPG fleet, not run now. PagerLens's instance has edb_stat_monitor, query_advisor, tcn and pgvector installed. And query_advisor already suggests an index for the alert table. It only suggests. Nothing is changed.
12. Try it yourself. Three commands: migrate, seed, serve. Then open the Lab, and press Break it. The guide is in the docs folder. Section fifteen lists every EDB and AgentPG capability this app uses. Next, try the slow query scenario, and the Database tab. The end card repeats the legend: platform, scaffolding, sample app. Thanks for watching.

## 4. Recording notes (the Playwright recorder)

`docs/video/record.spec.ts` performs every on-screen action in real Google Chrome with Playwright video (1920 x 1080, light theme): a visible cursor, smooth scrolling, typed text in the terminal cards, callouts and lower thirds. Each scene is held for its narration plus 1.2 seconds and every action waits for the sentence it illustrates (`b.at('text of the sentence')`; a sentence that is not in `narration.txt` fails the run).

**What it checks while it records** (the run fails if the product and this script disagree): five service cards; the Rules top hypothesis is the bad deploy; the AI run is labelled "rules + AI" with AI turns; the second report lists the first incident as resolved as `bad_deploy`; the index definitions contain the HNSW index; the plan (sequential scans off) uses `incident_embedding`; the similarity lookup returns the confirmed incident; `search_runbooks` returns runbooks; `tcn` sends notifications for the incident and the alert, and the new row appears within 6 seconds without a reload; the `/metrics` gauge exists; the slow query evidence line appears; the read-only role sees `[EMAIL]` and no address, and is denied the base table, a write and `api_token`; the captures contain `edb_stat_monitor`, `query_advisor` and the suggested index.

**Not covered:** the AgentPG-only Database panels are not recorded (only CLI captures), nothing is run against AgentPG itself, and the narration is synthetic (replace it with a human recording for release). The simulator's alert fingerprints mean a second take on the same database would change the incident numbers: `build.sh` always starts from a fresh database.
