## 2 am_michael
Who is who? PagerLens is the sample app.

Three things around it are scaffolding you can swap: sign-in, the model gateway, and the secret vault.

Sign-in works with any standard OpenID Connect provider, as long as it sends the roles our apps expect.

The gateway is any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

The vault is one small module of the shared kit. Its key comes from the platform, so you replace it through the kit, not through a setting.

The platform part is Postgres on AgentPG, with its extensions.

## 9 af_heart
Now a quieter failure. No deploy, no outage. One query got slower.

Every app on the kit exports a gauge of its slowest statements. On AgentPG it comes from edb_stat_monitor.

On this local Postgres it comes from pg_stat_statements. This is the real gauge.

PagerLens's agent scrapes it. When a statement gets three times slower than its history, it opens a slow query alert.

The Lab scenario writes the points that the agent would have pushed. I break it, and investigate.

The top cause is a query that became forty times slower.

The evidence names the query, with its mean before and now.

The statement text itself is in the Database tab, under that query id.

## 10 am_eric
Now, who may read this data? An assistant should see incidents, but not personal data or secrets.

On AgentPG, a read-only user comes from one command. It is a member of a read-only group role. Locally, I created a role set up the same way.

As the owner, a note holds an email address.

As the read-only role, the masked view shows a placeholder instead.

The base table is denied. A write is denied too.

And the tables with secrets, like token hashes, are denied, with no view at all.

Postgres enforces this, not the application code.
